# Draw Me One — Visual Direction from Artist Reference Works

2026-11-16 — These notes reflect four user-supplied reference collages, which are **not licensed for publication on the website**. Do not upload the images, trace them, crop them, or include them in any GitHub asset. They were supplied strictly to understand the artist's style.

## Observations from references

1. Mixed traditional coloured-pencil character sketches and design studies (character poses, expressive faces, hands, red rose) on visible paper texture.
2. Finished digital character portraits featuring mood, richer shading, sensitive facial expressions and creative lighting.
3. Bright expressive anime/OC portraits, some linework and graphite-sketch studies with very individualized character design.
4. Detailed monochrome ink drawings and blue/pink mixed-media character studies with textured linework.

These are not generic mass-produced prints or an agency's catalog. Some are polished fully-coloured portraits; others are exploratory and intentionally handmade.

## Desired emotional tone

**Personal, thoughtful, a little playful, capable, intimate and artist-led.** Avoid huge agency typography, overbearing marketing copy, generic "premium" corporate grids, glassy dashboards on the public site, mechanical faux-sales badges, aggressive neon, image-generation terminology or stock-photo aesthetics.

## Design priorities

- Studio is the only layout currently close to acceptable. **Do not keep endlessly refining Atelier / Edition / Signature**; either retire them from public selection or replace them later with genuinely different artist-appropriate concepts after Studio is established.
- Explore two later ideas only as optional test variants: **Midnight Sketchbook** (charcoal, warm paper, muted lavender or burgundy, gentle type) and **Artist's Desk** (personal process, sketchbook notes, sketch-to-finished comparison without fake texture obscuring the drawings).
- Human scale: restrained H1 size, tighter navigation, modest spacing, clear breathing room around drawings, avoid 100vh hero unless warranted.
- Artwork leads the visual hierarchy; preserve its colors and aspect ratios in gallery and focus viewer. Prefer photo-real actual work once approved, not abstract SVG pretend-portfolio content.
- Light/dark available to visitors; default probably dark. Palette, published page layout, gallery display, logo and content all admin controlled.
- Scroll gallery remains a candidate favorite; test it alongside masonry with real portrait ratios, without changing user's original art.
- CTA: "Commission a drawing" or similarly personal, followed by an estimate and artist-review process, not immediate forced checkout.
- Personal About section with artist-approved words; plain-language policies and accurate examples. Avoid fabricated statistics, testimonials or claims about turnaround/volume.
- Layer in micro-interactions with reduced-motion fallback. High quality achieved by care and polish rather than excess decoration.
